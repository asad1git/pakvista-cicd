Karachi – City of Lights

Pakistan's largest city — a coastal metropolis of culture, commerce, and contrasts.

About Karachi

Karachi is Pakistan's largest city and its financial capital, home to over 16 million people and a sprawling coastline on the Arabian Sea. Known as the "City of Lights," Karachi is a city that never truly sleeps — its streets buzz with commerce, its beaches fill at sunset, and its restaurants serve cuisine that reflects centuries of cultural exchange between Sindhi, Baloch, Muhajir, Punjabi, and Pashtun communities.

Founded as a small fishing village in the early 18th century and developed into a major port by the British, Karachi grew exponentially after the Partition of 1947, when it became the first capital of Pakistan. Today it is a city of extraordinary contrasts: gleaming skyscrapers rise beside colonial-era buildings, luxury malls sit minutes from ancient bazaars, and a vibrant arts scene flourishes alongside a deeply traditional way of life.

Top Attractions

Clifton Beach

Clifton Beach is Karachi's most beloved public space — a long stretch of sandy shoreline along the Arabian Sea where residents gather at sunset to walk, ride horses and camels, eat street food, and simply breathe in the sea air. The beach is flanked by the upscale Clifton and Defence neighbourhoods and offers views of the open sea on one side and the colourful chaos of Karachi's leisure culture on the other. The adjoining Sea View area features food stalls, funfairs, and a promenade that comes alive in the cooler months. For many Karachiites, Clifton Beach is the city's living room — a place of escape, community, and evening ritual.

Mohatta Palace

Built in 1927 by the wealthy merchant Shivratan Chandraratan Mohatta as a summer residence, Mohatta Palace is one of the most beautiful buildings in Karachi and a remarkable example of an architectural style that blends Rajputana, Mughal, and Indo-Saracenic influences. Constructed from pink Jodhpur stone and yellow Gizri stone, the palace is distinguished by its ornate arched windows, domed turrets, and intricate latticework. After Partition, the palace was used by the Government of Pakistan and later as a residence for Fatima Jinnah, sister of the nation's founder. Today it functions as a museum and art gallery, housing rotating exhibitions of Pakistani art and the personal belongings of Fatima Jinnah.

Pakistan Maritime Museum

The Pakistan Maritime Museum, located in the PNS Himalaya naval base in Karachi, is a fascinating tribute to Pakistan's naval history and its connection to the sea. Established in 1988, the museum houses an impressive collection of maritime artefacts, ship models, weapons, navigational instruments, and memorabilia tracing the history of the Pakistan Navy from its founding in 1947 to the present day. The centrepiece of the museum is the PNS Ghazi, a decommissioned submarine that visitors can board and explore — one of the most unique tourist experiences in Karachi. The museum also displays a retired warship, aircraft, guns, and an extensive collection of medals and uniforms.

A City Like No Other

Karachi defies easy description. It is simultaneously ancient and ultra-modern, chaotic and organised, overwhelming and deeply welcoming. It is the city where Pakistan's economic engine runs, where its most diverse communities coexist, and where the Arabian Sea provides a constant reminder of the wider world beyond. To visit Karachi is to experience Pakistan in its most complex and compelling form.
