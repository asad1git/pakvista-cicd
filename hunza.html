Hunza Valley

A kingdom of turquoise lakes, ancient forts, and peaks that pierce the sky.

About Hunza Valley

Nestled in the Karakoram mountain range in the northernmost region of Pakistan, Hunza Valley is one of the most dramatically beautiful places on earth. At an elevation of roughly 2,400 metres above sea level, the valley is flanked by colossal peaks — including Rakaposhi (7,788 m) and Ultar Sar (7,388 m) — and threaded by the mighty Hunza River. For centuries, Hunza was an independent princely state, and its people are renowned for their longevity, literacy, and hospitality.

The valley offers a rare combination of natural wonder and historical depth. Terraced apricot orchards cling to steep hillsides, ancient forts crown rocky outcrops, and glacial lakes shimmer in impossible shades of blue and green. The Karakoram Highway, one of the highest paved international roads in the world, links Hunza to the rest of Pakistan and offers a journey of extraordinary scenic grandeur.

Top Attractions

Rakaposhi Viewpoint

Rakaposhi, meaning "Shining Wall" in the local Burushaski language, is one of the most visually striking mountains on earth. Its unbroken south face rises nearly 6,000 metres in a single sweep from the valley floor — a vertical relief that makes it one of the most dramatic mountain walls anywhere. The viewpoint near Minapin village, accessible via a short drive from the Karakoram Highway, offers a spectacular panorama of the peak reflected in the surrounding landscape. At dawn and dusk, when the snow glows pink and orange, the view is simply unforgettable.

Attabad Lake

Attabad Lake was formed in January 2010 when a massive landslide blocked the Hunza River, creating a vast body of water over the next five months that submerged villages and a stretch of the Karakoram Highway. What was once a tragedy has since become one of Pakistan's most photographed natural attractions. The lake stretches approximately 21 kilometres and glows an extraordinary shade of turquoise owing to its glacial origins and mineral content. Visitors can take boat rides across the lake, passing through dramatic mountain scenery and the tunnels built to replace the submerged highway section.

Baltit Fort

Perched dramatically on a rocky ridge above the town of Karimabad, Baltit Fort is one of the oldest and most important historical monuments in the Karakoram region. Built over 700 years ago and subsequently modified by Tibetan craftsmen in the 14th and 16th centuries, the fort served as the residence of the Mirs (rulers) of Hunza for many generations. It was donated to the Aga Khan Cultural Service in 1945 and has since been meticulously restored. The fort offers breathtaking views of the valley, the Ultar glacier, and on clear days, a sweep of snow-capped peaks stretching to the horizon.

The Roof of the World

Hunza sits at the confluence of three of the world's greatest mountain ranges — the Karakoram, the Himalayas, and the Hindu Kush. Nowhere else on earth can you stand in a valley and look up at so many giants of the natural world. It is a place that reduces the visitor to a respectful silence and leaves an impression that lasts a lifetime.
