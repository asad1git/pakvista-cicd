Lahore – City of Gardens

Where Mughal grandeur meets modern energy — the cultural heart of Pakistan.

About Lahore

Lahore is Pakistan's second-largest city and its cultural capital, renowned for its rich Mughal heritage, stunning architecture, and legendary cuisine. With a history stretching back over a thousand years, Lahore has served as the seat of empires and the cradle of poets, musicians, and artists. The city pulses with an energy that is uniquely its own — the scent of street food mingling with the call to prayer, the glow of ancient minarets against a modern skyline.

Visitors to Lahore discover a city that wears its history proudly while embracing the present. The old walled city is a labyrinth of bazaars, havelis, and mosques; just beyond its gates, wide boulevards and colonial-era buildings speak of a different era. Lahore is a city that demands to be explored slowly, on foot, and with an appetite for both history and flavour.

Top Attractions

Badshahi Mosque

Commissioned by the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb and completed in 1673, the Badshahi Mosque is one of the most iconic landmarks of the Islamic world. Built from red sandstone and decorated with intricate marble inlays, it can accommodate up to 100,000 worshippers in its vast courtyard. The mosque stands opposite the Lahore Fort, together forming the centrepiece of Lahore's Mughal heritage quarter. At night, when the mosque is illuminated, it presents one of the most spectacular sights in all of South Asia.

Lahore Fort (Shahi Qila)

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Lahore Fort is a massive complex of palaces, mosques, and gardens that served as the royal residence of Mughal emperors from Akbar to Aurangzeb. Spread over 20 hectares, the fort contains 21 notable monuments including the famous Sheesh Mahal — the Palace of Mirrors — whose walls and ceilings are encrusted with thousands of tiny mirrors and coloured glass that shimmer brilliantly in candlelight. The Diwan-i-Aam (Hall of Public Audience) and the Naulakha Pavilion are equally breathtaking examples of Mughal artistry.

Shalimar Gardens

Laid out in 1641 by the Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan, the Shalimar Gardens are a magnificent example of Mughal garden design, featuring three terraced levels of fountains, marble pavilions, and flowering plants. Also a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the gardens once served as a royal retreat and a venue for imperial celebrations. The central terrace, known as the Farah Bakhsh (Bestower of Pleasure), contains 105 fountains surrounding a central platform where the emperor would hold court. The gardens remain one of Lahore's most serene and beautiful spaces.

Lahore is Lahore

"Lahore is Lahore" — a common Pakistani saying that captures the city's irreplaceable character. No other city in Pakistan combines the same depth of history, richness of culture, warmth of people, and abundance of flavour. A visit to Lahore is, in many ways, a visit to the soul of Pakistan.
