Skardu – Gateway to the Karakoram

Where the world's mightiest mountains meet ancient deserts and sapphire lakes.

About Skardu

Skardu is the capital of the Skardu District in Gilgit-Baltistan, situated at an altitude of approximately 2,228 metres above sea level where the Indus and Shigar rivers meet. It serves as the primary base camp for mountaineers attempting K2 — the world's second highest peak — as well as several other eight-thousanders including Broad Peak, Gasherbrum I, and Gasherbrum II. Yet Skardu is far more than a mountaineering hub; it is a destination of extraordinary natural beauty in its own right.

The town is surrounded by a dramatic landscape of sweeping sand dunes, cold desert plateaus, glacial lakes of impossible blue, and valleys carved by ancient rivers over millions of years. The people of Skardu — predominantly Balti, a community with Tibetan cultural roots — are known for their warmth, their skill in traditional crafts, and their deep connection to the mountains that define their lives. For any traveller seeking the truly untamed and awe-inspiring, Skardu is an essential destination.

Top Attractions

Shangrila Resort & Kachura Lakes

Lower Kachura Lake, popularly known as Shangrila Lake, is one of the most photographed and celebrated natural attractions in all of Pakistan. Located approximately 32 kilometres from Skardu town, the lake sits at an altitude of 2,500 metres and is ringed by weeping willows, poplar trees, and flowering gardens that reflect perfectly in its mirror-still turquoise waters. The Shangrila Resort, built on the lake's shore in the 1980s, features a restaurant housed inside the fuselage of a vintage Dakota aircraft — one of the most unique dining experiences in the country. Upper Kachura Lake, a short hike above, is even more pristine and offers a more secluded and serene experience for those willing to walk.

Deosai National Park

Deosai, meaning "Land of Giants" in the local Shina language, is one of the most extraordinary landscapes on earth. At an average elevation of 4,114 metres above sea level, it is the world's second highest plateau — a vast, treeless expanse of rolling grassland that stretches over 3,000 square kilometres along the border of Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Kashmir. During summer (June to September), Deosai erupts into a breathtaking carpet of wildflowers — hundreds of species blooming in every colour — while streams and rivers run silver across the golden plain. Deosai is also the last significant habitat of the Himalayan brown bear in Pakistan, and the national park was established in 1993 specifically to protect this endangered species. The Sheosar Lake at the heart of the plateau, surrounded by green hills and reflecting the open sky, is among the most beautiful sights in Pakistan.

Kharpocho Fort

Perched dramatically atop a massive granite rock above Skardu town, Kharpocho Fort — whose name means "King of Forts" in Balti — commands breathtaking views of the Skardu Valley, the Indus River, and the surrounding mountain ranges. The fort's origins date back to the 8th century, though most of the existing structure was built by Ali Sher Khan Anchan, the ruler of Baltistan, in the 16th century. The fort served as the seat of power for the Baltistan kingdom and witnessed centuries of battles, alliances, and royal intrigue. Though partially ruined, the fort retains its majestic presence, and the climb to its summit — either by a winding path or rough steps — rewards visitors with one of the most spectacular panoramic views in all of Gilgit-Baltistan. At sunset, when the Indus valley turns gold and the mountains glow purple in the fading light, Kharpocho Fort is a place of rare and moving beauty.

Land of Giants

Skardu occupies a unique place among Pakistan's destinations — it is at once a town, a wilderness, and a gateway to the extreme. Nowhere else can you wake up to sand dunes, drive to a glacial lake by afternoon, and fall asleep under a sky blazing with stars at high altitude. For the adventurer, the photographer, the historian, and the dreamer, Skardu offers a scale of beauty and drama that is matched by very few places on earth.
